Campaign Chronicle

A causal event record for economic, logistical, political, faction, and corporate developments. Detailed archive history is retained within a configurable campaign-wide byte budget (512 KB by default); oldest detail is pruned first while aggregate counters and settlement idempotency records remain. Download periodic GM JSON backups for long campaigns.

Diplomatic relations

Bilateral relationships can be adjusted directly. Trade flows, transit incidents, and hostile engagements now produce bounded automatic pressure on unlocked relationships. GM-authored locks remain authoritative.

Strategic intelligence and decisions

Factions score viable actions against their goals, assets, resources, market dependencies, threats, and diplomacy. With political autonomy enabled, faction actors can propose limited trade/transit/non-aggression agreements, respond to older offers, and seek ceasefire or peace under pressure; the GM can disable this separately. Corporations compare rival supply offers using expected delivered cost, including observed delivery reliability, before committing to a new contract. Decisions include explanations and uncertainty; GM controls and manual locks remain authoritative. Polity-to-polity diplomacy remains referee-directed.

Faction asset roster

Review and edit individual assets, upkeep, hit points, deployment, and readiness.

Faction turns track income, upkeep, maintenance, asset purchases, influence, repair, and attack events using Sector Forge’s original generic asset catalog. This is a playable campaign aid, not a verbatim reproduction of the full published faction asset catalog and all special asset abilities.

Territorial influence

Control is recalculated from home systems and deployed assets. A referee override can settle contested borders.

World markets

Markets track local production, consumption, inventories, shortages, and prices. Search by world, system, faction control, commodity, category, or tag. Goods tables and editing controls are built only when you open one market.

Interstellar logistics

Shipments move over operational trade routes, consume route capacity, take time to arrive, and can be delayed or lose cargo to transit incidents. Diplomatic policies can restrict or price access across controlled borders. Commodity-aware cargo, recurring supply contracts, freight, tolls, tariffs, operator costs, and delivered commodity purchases now settle through persistent campaign-credit accounts.

Transport infrastructure

Spaceports constrain cargo handling. Commissioned fleets add route capacity, influence transit speed and reliability, and require recurring operating costs. Routes without assigned fleets retain their abstract baseline capacity for compatibility.

Fleets and route assignments

Recurring supply contracts

Define recurring supply commitments between two markets. Contracts can set a negotiated fixed price, monthly-turn volume, and priority; they never bypass route capacity, cargo losses, market storage, or diplomatic restrictions.

Commodity freight profiles

Cargo now consumes route capacity according to its freight units per lot. Defaults reflect broad handling classes; the GM can override each commodity profile for the sector.

Formal political agreements

Propose and ratify treaties, impose or lift sanctions, and record war, ceasefire, and peace transitions. Active policies are applied to the relevant route borders during logistics planning.

Commercial finances

Abstract campaign-credit accounts track faction treasuries, corporate capital, freight operators, and customs receipts. Opening capital is an estimated campaign baseline, not a published SWN conversion; balances and overdraft warning thresholds are referee-facing; the threshold flags debt but does not prevent a transfer.
